AI USAGE

1 AUTHORSHIP AND DIRECTION

Tang Ziyi wrote the original EPQ paper, conceived the initial formalisation,
and constructed initial formal objects. The research question, choice
of Isabelle/ZF, and direction of the project originated with the author.
Tang Ziyi has led and participated in the work throughout, setting its scope,
examining the mathematical statements, and directing revisions. The author
retains responsibility for the definitions, claims, and published material.

2 TOOL PARTICIPATION

OpenAI Codex and ChatGPT have assisted development since August 2026. Their
participation includes proposed and retained definitions, theorem statements,
proof scripts, documentation, and build infrastructure. These contributions
are part of the maintained development and are subject to its verification
requirements.

Aristotle and Anthropic Claude participated in internal review and
cross-checking under the author's direction. Aristotle also supplied draft
proofs and proposed revisions, which were assessed against the maintained
Isabelle sources. These activities are project checks and do not constitute
external peer review or endorsement by AFP or ITP.

3 REVIEW AND VERIFICATION

The collaboration follows the repository rules in AGENTS.md and
CONTRIBUTING.txt. The author directs the research and reviews its statements
and revisions throughout development. This participation is distinct from
personally writing or inspecting every line of every generated proof script.
Git history records retained changes. Verification records identify the
source revision, proof checks, and remaining limitations.

Isabelle checks derivability from the recorded definitions and assumptions.
This does not by itself establish that a definition expresses the intended
mathematics, that a result is novel, or that the full EPQ argument has been
formalised. AI review and machine proof checking do not replace the author's
mathematical judgment or independent expert review.

This disclosure follows the transparency and human responsibility principles
in Dagstuhl Publishing's Statement on the Use of Generative Artificial
Intelligence, applicable to the LIPIcs series in which ITP proceedings are
published. It identifies the tools' purposes and the author's role. It does
not assert approval by a conference or publisher.
